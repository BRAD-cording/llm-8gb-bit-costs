\begin{abstract}
Quantisation fits a large language model into an 8\,GB device, and the literature
optimises it for speed while spending its bits on the weights. On this device
class both choices are wrong, and this paper replaces them with a measured
decision. Three inputs decide the weight/cache split, and none of them is
reported in the work that asks the question. The \emph{objective} is capacity,
not latency: 4-bit weights buy little or no time because dequantisation is not
fused into the matmul. The \emph{exchange rate} runs against practice: with
published implementations on both sides, one cache bit costs $9.0\times$ less
accuracy than one weight bit on Qwen3-0.6B and $13.3\times$ on 1.7B
($11.39\%$ against $1.27\%$ perplexity, $3.20\%$ against $0.24\%$), the ordering
reproduces on WikiText-2, and a 2-bit passkey experiment locates where it stops.
The \emph{budget} is device-dependent: the usable-memory fraction is $0.133$ on a
discrete-GPU laptop and $0.419$ on a unified-memory edge module, a $3.15\times$
difference in the fraction lost, which moves the price of a weight bit by
$3.7\times$. The allocator that follows is textbook, and we say so; the inputs
are not, and on both devices the resulting policy compresses the cache and
leaves the weights at 8 bits. Two further measurements bound the claim: the
serving stack sets the budget before any bit does --- grouped-query attention
silently disables every fused attention kernel, and materialising the KV heads
cuts attention peak memory $32\times$ and 4k prefill time $5.8\times$ --- and at
2 bits the cache quantisation scheme decides long-context retrieval where at 4
bits it does not.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Highlights}
\begin{itemize}
\item 4-bit weights buy no time on this stack; the objective must be capacity, not speed.
\item One cache bit costs 9--13x less accuracy than one weight bit, with published quantisers.
\item Usable memory differs 3.15x across two 8\,GB devices; no published split transfers.
\item The optimal policy compresses the cache, leaves weights at 8 bits, and we execute it.
\item The serving stack, not the bit width, sets the budget: 32x attention peak memory.
\end{itemize}
